# GEX dealer-positioning — five-year results (Phase 6)

Run of 2026-09-04 against the full re-built `option_metrics` / `ml_dataset`.
Raw output: `models/evaluation/gex_h1.json`. Reproduce with `uv run mktmtm test-gex`.

> **Sign convention corrected 2026-09-04 (commit `9ef7be7`); this page is stated
> under the corrected convention and was regenerated by `mktmtm gex-features &&
> mktmtm test-gex` on 2026-09-04.** `DEALER_SIGN` was the US default (dealers long
> calls, short puts). NSE participant-wise OI measures the opposite for single
> stocks — the Pro book is net short calls on 99.9% of 1,258 days and net long puts
> on 98.3% — so the constant is now `{"CE": -1.0, "PE": +1.0}` and `gex` is negated
> throughout. See `docs/data/participant-oi-report.md`.
>
> The correction was verified to be a pure negation: against the pre-correction
> parquet, `gex` equals `-gex` on all 212,441 rows, while `|gex|` and
> `dist_to_flip` are identical on all of them (194,070 for the latter). Every
> sign-invariant statistic below is therefore unchanged to five decimals from the
> 2026-09-02 run; only signed quantities moved.
>
> **Index options are not covered by this constant.** The Pro index book flipped
> sign monotonically across 2024-25 (net short calls on 90.5% of days in 2021,
> 36.7% in 2026), so no fixed value is right for them. Indices are 3,086 of
> 212,441 rows and are excluded from dealer-exposure claims rather than assigned
> a sign.

**Data.** 212,441 symbol-days, 217 symbols, 1,254 trading dates, 2021-08-02 → 2026-08-28.
Per-strike `gamma`/`vanna`/`charm` present on 100% of chain elements; `gamma_flip`
resolves on 91.4% of rows (the remainder have no zero crossing in the ±10% band,
which is a real state, not a failure). Under the corrected sign `gex` is **negative on 206,553
symbol-days (97.3%)**, positive on 5,634, zero on 11 — dealers are short gamma
almost always, which is what a market where retail buys calls and dealers write
them should look like.

Target throughout: `next_day_abs_return` (adjusted basis).

## Headline: H1 fails, and under the corrected sign it fails twice over

H1 — *dealers short gamma (GEX < 0) precedes higher realized volatility* — fails
its own pooled test under the corrected convention, and separately fails both
confound controls. Either failure alone would be sufficient.

| test | result | reading |
|---|---|---|
| **H1 Welch, gex<0 vs gex>0** | **1.360% vs 1.547%, t = +8.96, p = 4.3e−19** | **fails — the sign runs the wrong way** |
| IC(−gex), cross-sectional | −0.0674, t = −22.97, ICIR −0.65, hit 25.9% | fails |
| flip `DEALER_SIGN` (= the old, wrong convention) | IC +0.0674, h1_consistent True | reproduces the discarded result |
| liquidity filter (volume > 0 strikes) | IC −0.0676, t = +8.13 | fails (stable) |
| expiry week | IC −0.0690 | fails (stable) |
| indices only | Welch t = −0.36, p = 0.72 | no effect either way |
| **sign vs magnitude** | **IC(signed) −0.0674 vs IC(−\|gex\|) +0.0723** | **the sign is not what predicts** |
| **partial IC \| `ce_iv_30`** | **+0.0099, t = +3.35** vs `ce_iv_30` alone +0.2114 | **no incremental information** |

**1. The direction is backwards.** Dealers are short gamma on 97.3% of symbol-days,
and the *rare long-gamma days are the volatile ones* (1.547% vs 1.360%). Standard
dealer-hedging theory says the opposite: long gamma means dealers sell rallies and
buy dips, damping realized volatility. The pooled statistic is large and highly
significant — it simply points the other way.

**2. The sign carries no information regardless.** Discarding it entirely and
ranking on `−|gex|` gives IC +0.0723, better than the signed version at either
polarity (|−0.0674|). H1 is a claim about the sign, and `sign_adds_information` is
`False`. GEX ∝ ΣOI · Γ · S², so it scales with a name's size and option liquidity;
small, thinly-traded names carry small |GEX| and are more volatile for reasons
unrelated to dealer hedging. The pooled test was ranking symbols by size.

**3. Implied volatility already contains it.** `ce_iv_30` alone scores IC +0.2114,
three times the magnitude of the GEX IC. Residualize the GEX level against it and
the partial IC is +0.0099 — statistically distinguishable from zero (t = +3.35) at
this sample size, but economically negligible against a 0.2114 baseline.

The index result is consistent with all three: dealer-hedging theory should apply
most strongly to NIFTY/BANKNIFTY, where hedging flow is most concentrated. There
the effect is absent (p = 0.72). It is now clear why — the Pro index book changed
sign mid-sample, so no fixed convention describes it.

**Consequence for the supervised model.** The Phase-6 model reports fixed-split
IC 0.2358 (ICIR 1.92, t = 30.2) and walk-forward fold ICs from 0.196. Given that
`ce_iv_30` alone scores 0.2114, essentially all of that is the IV feature. The
model is not evidence for the dealer-positioning hypothesis and must not be
presented as such.

## What does survive: a magnitude effect, within symbol

Re-specifying H1 *within* each symbol removes the size confound structurally — a
symbol is only ever compared with itself. Each symbol's `gex` is z-scored against
its own past using an expanding mean/std over strictly prior rows (`shift(1)`,
min 60 observations), so the statistic is causal.

| | value |
|---|---|
| rows (after z-score warm-up) | 199,178 |
| Spearman(own gex z, own next-day \|ret\|) | **+0.0748** |
| own-lowest-decile gex, mean \|ret\| | 1.232% |
| own-highest-decile gex, mean \|ret\| | 1.614% |
| decile ratio / Welch | **0.76×** (1.31× inverted), t = −26.93, p = 2.8e−158 |
| partial Spearman, controlling own IV z | **+0.0436** |

Per-year: 2021 +0.060, 2022 +0.087, 2023 +0.020, 2024 +0.046, 2025 +0.083,
2026 +0.078. Same sign in all six years; weakest in 2023.

`h1_consistent` is `False` here too, and that label is now the useful part: read
as a *directional dealer-gamma* claim this fails, because the correlation points
the wrong way for hedging theory.

Read as a **magnitude** claim it survives intact, and this is the defensible
result. Because `gex` is negative on 97.3% of rows, a *higher* (less negative)
own-`gex` z-score means a *smaller* |GEX| — less dealer gamma outstanding. So the
finding is:

> **When a symbol carries unusually little dealer gamma relative to its own
> history, its next-day move is larger — and that holds after controlling for the
> symbol's own implied volatility.**

That statement is invariant to the sign convention, which is why it is the one
worth keeping. It is also exactly what `sign_vs_magnitude` says cross-sectionally
(|GEX| beats signed GEX), arrived at independently. Unlike the pooled test it
cannot be explained by size, liquidity, or price level, because those are constant
within a symbol over the comparison.

The effect is modest (ρ = 0.044 after the IV control) and is a volatility
statement, not a directional one.

## Dealer features that retain incremental value over IV

Partial IC against `ce_iv_30`, cross-sectional, full sample. Signs are as reported
under the corrected convention; `dist_to_flip` and `otm_put_oi_build` do not carry
`DEALER_SIGN` and so are directly comparable with the pre-correction run.

| feature | partial IC | t | carries `DEALER_SIGN`? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `dist_to_flip` | **+0.0210** | +7.47 | no — invariant |
| `charm_exp` | +0.0156 | +4.40 | yes |
| `dex` | +0.0124 | +3.94 | **no — see limitations** |
| `vanna_exp` | −0.0120 | −3.37 | yes |
| `gex_chg` | −0.0110 | −4.06 | yes |
| `−gex` (level) | +0.0099 | +3.35 | yes |
| `otm_put_oi_build` | +0.0063 | +2.26 | no — invariant |

`dist_to_flip` — distance from spot to the gamma-flip level — is the strongest,
and it is invariant under the sign correction (negating net GEX does not move its
zeros), so it is the one dealer feature whose value did not depend on a convention
that turned out to be wrong. It is also the feature that most directly encodes the
dealer-gamma *mechanism* rather than exposure size. The GEX level is near the
bottom. That ordering is itself evidence: what predicts is proximity to the regime
boundary, not how much gamma is outstanding.

All of these are small against `ce_iv_30` alone at +0.2114 (t = +48.5).

## Known limitations

- **The dealer side is measured for stocks, undetermined for indices.** The
  correction rests on NSE participant-wise OI, which is published by instrument
  *category* (index vs single stock), not per symbol. So the convention is
  validated at the aggregate level and applied uniformly within the single-stock
  universe; a per-symbol dealer sign remains unavailable. For indices no fixed
  sign is correct at all — see the note at the top.
- **`dex` does not carry `DEALER_SIGN`, though its siblings do.** `ml/dataset.py:177`
  computes `SUM(oi · delta · S)` with no sign term, while `gex`, `gex_chg`,
  `vanna_exp` and `charm_exp` all apply it. This faithfully implements the spec
  (`docs/implementation/gex-dealer-positioning.md:250`), but that spec describes
  the quantity as "net delta dealers must hedge" — an interpretation that requires
  the sign. As computed, `dex` is the OI-weighted net delta of the chain, not a
  dealer position, and it did not move under the correction. Unresolved: fixing it
  is a design decision, not a bug fix, and would change the reported partial IC.
- **Open interest is in contracts, not shares.** Lot sizes are applied from
  `data/reference/lot_sizes.csv`; any gap in that schedule mis-scales exposure
  for the affected symbol-periods.
- **Settlement IV contamination.** Untraded deep strikes carry settlement-derived
  IV. Measured: ATM ±5% carries 83.1% of the gamma×OI weight, and strikes beyond
  30% moneyness carry 0.0%, so the contamination sits where it has no weight. The
  liquidity-filtered robustness check (volume > 0 strikes only) is unchanged at
  IC +0.0676.
- **`interest_rate` is unusable as a feature.** The ETL carries the MIFOR rate
  forward across gaps (e.g. 11 days around 2025-04-15); it is a frozen constant
  on those dates.
- **5 catalog symbols produce no rows**: FINNIFTY and MIDCPNIFTY have no working
  Yahoo ticker (documented, expected); LTIM, PEL and TATAMOTORS are new to this
  run and not yet diagnosed.
